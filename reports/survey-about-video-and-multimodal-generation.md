# Video and Multimodal Generation: A Survey of Foundations, Methods, and Frontiers

## TL;DR

- Text-to-video generation has evolved from early GAN-based approaches using spatio-temporal convolutions to modern diffusion transformer (DiT) architectures with billions of parameters, such as HunyuanVideo (13B) [1] and Movie Gen (30B) [2], which now match or exceed closed-source models.
- Autoregressive LLM-style video generation treats images, videos, text, and audio as discrete tokens in a unified vocabulary, enabling zero-shot capability chaining and long-horizon generation up to 10 seconds [3].
- Multimodal conditioning systems increasingly replace standard text encoders with MLLMs and use joint embedding spaces (Lumina-T2X) that unify VAE latents across modalities, while native audio-video models like DreamX-Creator use Gated Cross-Modal Attention for synchronized generation [4][5][6].
- Controllable video generation has advanced toward sophisticated camera control via homography warping [7] and unified multi-modal control over scene, subject identity, and motion simultaneously [8].
- Evaluation has shifted from single-number metrics (FVD, FID) toward multi-dimensional benchmarks (VBench++) [9]; however, state-of-the-art models still complete less than 20% of temporal compositionality tasks on TC-Bench [10].

## Background

Video generation aims to produce temporally coherent, semantically meaningful moving images conditioned on inputs such as text prompts, reference images, or audio signals. The field traces its origins to generative adversarial networks adapted for the spatio-temporal domain, where early methods extended 2D generators with explicit spatio-temporal convolutions and three-discriminator architectures evaluating video-level, frame-level, and motion coherence. These early GAN-based approaches established that directly applying image synthesis to video yields temporally incoherent output, establishing spatio-temporal adversarial objectives as essential.

The paradigm shift arrived with diffusion models. Make-A-Video pioneered text-to-video generation without paired text-video data by extending a text-to-image U-Net with pseudo-3D convolution and factorized temporal attention layers initialized as identity mappings, generating 16 frames at 64×64 resolution interpolated to 76 frames then super-resolved to 768×768. ModelScopeT2V followed with a 1.7-billion-parameter model where 39% of UNet parameters were dedicated to temporal capabilities, employing spatio-temporal blocks that factorize convolution and attention over space and time, and multi-frame training on both LAION-5B image-text pairs (~1,400 batch size) and WebVid ~10M video-text pairs (~3,200 batch size).

Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) scaled latent video diffusion to unprecedented levels, requiring approximately 200,000 A100 80GB hours across 48×8 GPUs for training on a curated LVD-F dataset of 152 million examples. SVD generates 14–25 frames at 576×1024 resolution with two variants differing in frame count, demonstrating powerful motion representation learnable through fine-tuning for image-to-video, multi-view synthesis, and camera-motion-specific LoRA modules. CogVideo represented an alternative trajectory: a 9B-parameter autoregressive transformer inheriting pretrained text-to-image priors from CogView2, described as probably the first open-source large-scale pretrained text-to-video model, addressing the challenge that autoregressive transformers struggle with text-action relations due to scarcity of text-video datasets [3].

## Foundational Architectures

The dominant architectural families in video generation can be categorized into three groups: diffusion-based U-Net models, DiT-based diffusion models, and autoregressive LLM approaches.

**Diffusion U-Net models** remain widely deployed. ModelScopeT2V introduced temporal conv and attention outputs initialized as zeros, enabling temporally discontinuous frames at the start of training that progressively learn temporal correspondence — a technique adopted by many subsequent works. SVD employed a three-stage training pipeline: text-to-image pretraining, video pretraining on 152M examples at low resolution (256×384, 14 frames), and high-resolution finetuning on ~1M samples at 576×1024, using an EDM noise schedule shifted toward higher noise for high-resolution finetuning. Both models operate in latent space through a frozen VQGAN encoder, leveraging OpenCLIP text encoders for conditioning.

**Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models** have become the de facto standard for state-of-the-art generation. HunyuanVideo, an open-source model with over 13 billion parameters, achieves performance comparable to or superior to leading closed-source models including Runway Gen-3 and Luma 1.6 through a comprehensive framework integrating professional data curation, DiT architecture design, and progressive model scaling [1]. Movie Gen pushes this further with a 30B-parameter transformer and maximum context length of 73K video tokens (16 seconds at 16 fps), generating high-quality 1080p HD videos with synchronized audio [2]. Lumina-T2X demonstrates the scalability of DiT across heterogeneous output modalities — images, videos, multi-view 3D objects, and audio clips — all from a single Flag-DiT architecture with zero-initialized attention [5].

**Autoregressive LLM approaches** represent a fundamentally different paradigm. VideoPoet processes multimodal inputs (images, videos, text, audio) as discrete tokens in a unified vocabulary using MAGVIT-v2 video tokenizer and SoundStream audio tokenizer. Its two-stage training (pretraining with multimodal generative objectives, then task-specific adaptation) enables text-to-video, image-to-video, video editing, stylization, inpainting/outpainting, audio-to-video, and video-to-audio from a single model. It generates coherent long videos up to 10 seconds by autoregressively extending content conditioned on the last second of generated video, demonstrating strong object identity preservation and emergent controllable camera motion via text prompts [3].

A key trend across all families is the shift from pixel-space to latent-space generation, and from monolithic 3D operations to factorized spatiotemporal blocks. Early methods used full 3D convolutions, which are computationally prohibitive; modern approaches factorize spatial and temporal processing, either through pseudo-3D convolution (Make-A-Video) or decoupled attention. Scaling to billions of parameters — from CogVideo's 9B to Movie Gen's 30B — has been enabled by inheriting pretrained image-model weights and using massive text-image+video training corpora.

## Multimodal Conditioning

Modern video generation systems increasingly support rich multimodal conditioning, combining text, images, audio, and reference videos to achieve precise control over generation outcomes.

**Cross-modal conditioning strategies** vary significantly across architectures. Sora uses a Diffusion Transformer with decoupled spatial-temporal attention — spatial attention operates within frames while temporal attention operates across frames — with spatiotemporal prompt embeddings injected at every layer's cross-attention module and keyframe anchoring at specific layers (3, 7, 12). Pika employs hierarchical diffusion with optical flow guidance, where an upper diffusion network handles global structure and a lower flow-guidance module provides motion priors. Runway Gen-2 uses a condition UNet with CLIP text encoder aligned with ViT patch tokens via dynamic weight generation at decoder layers, trained through three-stage knowledge distillation (text→image→image→video→video) with progressive compression.

**Joint embedding spaces** unify representations across modalities. Lumina-T2X tokenizes the latent spatial-temporal space and incorporates learnable positional embeddings, transforming noise into images, videos, multi-view 3D objects, and audio clips conditioned on text instructions from a single Flag-DiT architecture [5]. X-Streamer takes this further as an end-to-end multimodal human world modeling framework with infinite interactions across text, speech, and video in a single unified architecture, using a Thinker-Actor dual-transformer design that perceives and reasons over streaming user inputs while generating responses in real-time video calls starting from a single portrait. LynnReal-Omni provides a unified multimodal video diffusion framework integrating agentic visual controls with high-fidelity generation and real-time acceleration for interactive workflows.

**Native audio-video generation** represents the latest frontier. DreamX-Creator jointly denoises modality-specialized audio and video streams in a compact 7B generator, with streams processed independently in the first half and coupled in the latter half through Gated Cross-Modal Attention using token- and head-wise output gates [4]. OmniSync achieves mask-free lip synchronization using Diffusion Transformers with dynamic guidance mechanisms, representing the shift from GAN-based to diffusion-based approaches for audio-driven face generation. RealTalk leverages a 3D facial prior-guided identity alignment network for precise lip synchronization and real-time facial rendering. ReSyncer produces high-fidelity lip-synced videos through a style-injected Transformer and re-configured Style-based generator.

**Multi-condition models** increasingly replace standard text encoders with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). LoomVideo replaces the standard text encoder with an MLLM in a 5B-parameter unified architecture for both video generation and editing, using deepstack injection to align multi-layer MLLM features with the DiT. It achieves zero-overhead Scale-and-Add conditioning for video editing by scaling and directly adding clean source video latent to noised target latent, eliminating token concatenation overhead and achieving at least 5.41× inference acceleration. Vorch-Human augments the conventional noisy forward process with long-horizon continuation for extended generation, spanning tasks including animating from driving speech, jointly generating speech+video from voice references, and synthesizing scenes from paired appearance+voice references [6].

The convergence across these approaches is clear: modality mixing improves controllability through layered injection, but requires careful gating to avoid temporal inconsistency. The move from separate per-modal models toward unified architectures (single trunk consuming all modalities) mirrors trends in vision-language models evolving from contrastive two-tower designs to native multimodal pretraining where image, video, audio, and text share a single neural network.

## Controllable Video Generation

Controllable video generation has evolved from simple style transfer to fine-grained spatial, temporal, and camera-level control.

**Camera control** has seen dramatic advances. AC3D optimizes pose conditioning schedules, selectively injects camera conditioning, and trains on diverse dynamic video datasets, demonstrating that careful conditioning injection strategies significantly improve controllability over camera motion without degrading visual quality. ActCam enables zero-shot video generation with joint control over character motion and camera parameters through geometrically consistent conditioning, using staged guidance in diffusion models to separately handle camera and subject motion while maintaining consistency. CamDirector addresses video trajectory editing by synthesizing new videos following user-defined camera paths while preserving scene content, using a hybrid warping scheme that progressively fuses static regions into a world cache then renders to target camera poses [7]. InfCam generates high-fidelity videos with accurate camera poses using infinite homography warping as conditioning signal, augmenting synthetic datasets with diverse trajectories to improve generalization.

**Spatial and subject control** is achieved through unified prompting frameworks. Tri-Prompting studies controllable video generation from three prompts: a first-frame scene image, 3D-aware multi-view subject references, and a motion-driving signal. It addresses the challenge where background regions are trackable under camera motion while foreground subjects can rotate, self-occlude, and reveal new regions requiring identity-consistent appearance [8]. FlexTraj introduces a unified point-based motion representation encoding each point with segmentation ID, temporally consistent trajectory ID, and optional color channel for appearance cues, enabling both dense and sparse trajectory control through efficient sequence-concatenation instead of token concatenation or ControlNet. LiON-LoRA proposes a LoRA fusion framework based on linear scalability, orthogonality, and norm consistency for unified spatial-temporal control, achieving precise linear controllability with only 4,000 iterations versus 10,000+ typically needed by competitors.

**Video editing and inpainting** have reached production maturity. VideoPainter is the first dual-branch video inpainting framework with plug-and-play control capabilities, featuring a lightweight context encoder comprising only 6% of backbone parameters, compatible with any pre-trained video DiT architectures and supporting various stylization backbones or LoRAs. It introduces inpainting region ID resampling technique to maintain ID consistency in any-length video inpainting and built VPData (>390K clips) and VPBench. MTV-Inpaint handles both traditional scene completion and novel object insertion within a single framework, designing dual-branch spatial attention in T2V diffusion U-Net and supporting multimodal control through I2V inpainting mode with a K2V (keyframe-to-video) two-stage pipeline.

Commercial systems have integrated these capabilities into production-grade tools. Runway Gen-4.5 ranked #1 on Artificial Analysis Text-to-Video benchmark with 1,247 Elo points as of November 2025, bringing all control modes (Image to Video, Keyframes, Video to Video) with significant improvements in temporal consistency and precise controllability. Luma Ray 3.2 offers multi-keyframe control allowing up to 16 individual keyframes within a single clip, enhanced performance tracking for skeletal poses, expressive facial capture for up to 8 faces, and Motion Transfer that extracts or preserves motion from a source video.

Foundational work like AnimateDiff established the pattern of plug-and-play motion modules trained once on real-world videos and seamlessly integrated into any personalized text-to-image model, introducing MotionLoRA for lightweight fine-tuning of motion patterns. ControlVideo demonstrated that ControlNet-style spatial conditioning can be effectively extended to the video domain without additional training, ensuring appearance coherence and reducing flicker. Despite these advances, challenges remain in long-horizon temporal coherence, precise causal editing (e.g., object removal with shadow/reflection handling), and mask-free fine-grained spatial control.

## Evaluation and Benchmarks

Evaluating video generation quality has evolved from single aggregate metrics to multi-dimensional, human-aligned assessment frameworks.

**Traditional automated metrics** have well-documented limitations. Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), introduced alongside StarCraft 2 Videos benchmark, extends FID by modeling spatiotemporal features using Inflated 3D Convnet (I3D) embeddings and became the widely adopted standard. However, STREAM demonstrates that FVD has stronger emphasis on spatial aspect than temporal naturalness and is constrained by input size of embedding networks to 16 frames, showing considerable instability diverging from human evaluations. EvalCrafter evaluated T2V models on four aspects using 17 objective metrics and found that existing metrics like FVD, IS, and CLIP similarity may perform well on prior T2I generation but do not adequately assess alignment with input text, motion quality, and temporal consistency for T2V. Feature-based automatic metrics (PIQE, BRISQUE, CLIP Score, X-CLIP-Score) show limited effectiveness across evaluation aspects.

**Disaggregated benchmark suites** decompose video quality into multiple dimensions. VBench proposes a top-down decomposition into 16 disentangled dimensions from two broad perspectives: Video Quality ("does the video alone look good?") and Video-Condition Consistency ("is the video consistent with what the user wants to generate?"). Temporal Quality includes five sub-dimensions: Subject Consistency (DINO feature similarity), Background Consistency (CLIP feature similarity), Temporal Flickering (mean absolute difference on static frames), Motion Smoothness (using frame interpolation model priors), and Dynamic Degree (RAFT optical flow estimation). VBench++ extends this to evaluate trustworthiness alongside intrinsic faithfulness challenges, expanding to commonsense reasoning, physics-based realism, human motion, and creative composition [9].

**MLLM-based evaluators** significantly outperform traditional metrics. VIDEOSCORE trained on 37.6K videos from 11 popular text-to-video models annotated by 20 expert raters achieves pairwise accuracy of 78.5% on GenAI-Bench, surpassing Gemini 1.5-Flash by 11.4 points, using both generative scoring (fixed text form output with regular expression extraction) and regression scoring (linear layer outputting 5 logits) trained on Mantis-Idefics2-8B accommodating 128 video frames. Video-Bench systematically leverages MLLMs across all dimensions relevant to video generation assessment, introducing chain-of-query (iterative multi-turn questioning) and few-shot scoring techniques; agreement with human scores averages 0.52 (on par with inter-rater agreement among humans), with chain-of-query improving Video-Condition Alignment by 0.093 over single-round approaches and few-shot scoring increasing correlation with human evaluations by 10.33% on average. VMBench identifies five perception-aligned dimensions (Perceptible Amplitude, Temporal Coherence, Object Integrity, Motion Smoothness, Commonsense Adherence) and shows rule-based metrics have limited discriminative power: AMT at 18.1%, Warping Error at -19.1% for motion smoothness.

**Physics realism evaluation** is an emerging frontier. PhyWorldBench evaluates adherence to laws of physics across fundamental principles (object motion, energy conservation) to complex scenarios (rigid body interactions, human/animal motion), testing 12 state-of-the-art models generating 12,600 videos across 1,050 curated prompts with Yes/No evaluation metric and large-scale human evaluation via Amazon Mechanical Turk. WorldModelBench evaluates world modeling capabilities with instruction-following and physics-adherence dimensions detecting subtle violations like irregular object size changes breaching mass conservation; crowdsourced 67K human labels measured 14 frontier models with a fine-tuned VLM judger achieving 8.6% higher average accuracy than GPT-4o with 2B parameters. Critically, correlation between VBench frame-wise quality win rates and WorldModelBench physics adherence drops to 0.28 (compared to 0.69 for overall correlation), showing VBench does not effectively distinguish videos based on physical laws — highest correlation between any VBench dimension and physics adherence is only 0.41 (aesthetic quality).

**Temporal compositionality** remains a major weakness. TC-Bench evaluates emergence of new concepts and relation transitions as time progresses through attribute transition, object relations, and background shifts scenarios, proposing TCR (Transition Completion Ratio) and TC-Score metrics using vision language models. Contemporary video generators accomplish less than approximately 20% of test cases, highlighting enormous improvement space [10]. CLIP similarity is unreliable here because training samples describe static images instead of transitions or motions, lacking awareness of compositional change across timesteps [10].

**Dataset resources** include OpenVid-1M, a million-level dataset with expressive captions containing over 1 million text-video pairs and 433K 1080p videos curated for HD video generation, prioritizing aesthetics, temporal consistency, and fluid motion through an automated pipeline.

## Trends and Open Problems

The landscape of video and multimodal generation has transformed dramatically between 2024 and 2026, characterized by several converging trends.

**Commercial scaling and capability expansion.** The 2024 era focused on producing coherent 4-second clips; the 2026 era focuses on controllability, character consistency, and longer durations. Sora 2 shipped with native audio generation, character consistency across multi-shot sequences via "cameos" feature, and 60-second-plus clips at 1080p, with audio generated alongside video in a single forward pass using a unified DiT architecture. Veo 3 leads on visual fidelity and physical-world realism, rumored to be trained with simulation-grounded data plus reinforcement signals, handling complex camera moves. Kling 2.0 produces excellent character motion and was the first commercial system to ship physically grounded long-form video supporting up to 2-minute clips. Usage statistics reflect explosive growth: Google's Veo model family reported over 275 million videos generated in roughly five months, OpenAI's Sora app hit 1 million downloads faster than ChatGPT reaching roughly 3 million daily active users on mobile, and across all AI video platforms monthly active users surpassed 124 million in January 2026. Sora achieves highest scores in 3D visual simulation (appearance score 0.8895, motion score 0.8394), indicating strong physics-aware generation.

**Efficiency breakthroughs.** Flow matching formulations demonstrate faster convergence than DDPM: Goku applies rectified flow formulation achieving lower FID-50K after 400k training steps compared to 1,000k steps required by DDPM equivalent. Pyramidal Flow Matching reinterprets denoising trajectory as pyramid stages where only final stage operates at full resolution, reducing training tokens from 119,040 to ≤15,360 for a 10-second 241-frame video and generating high-quality 5-second videos at 768p within 20.7k A100 GPU training hours. MaskFlow combines discrete representations with flow-matching using frame-level masking strategy during training, conditioning on previously generated unmasked frames to generate videos ten times beyond training sequences. DOLLAR achieves one-step distillation accelerating teacher model diffusion sampling by up to 278.6× enabling near real-time generation, with 4-step student model achieving VBench score of 82.57 surpassing teacher model using 50-step DDIM. MagicDistillation combines LoRA with Distribution Matching Distillation for few-step synthesis requiring only 4 steps and outperforming WanX-I2V (14B) and HunyuanVideo-I2V (13B) on VBench. Video-BLADE integrates Adaptive Block-Sparse Attention with Trajectory Distribution Matching step distillation, achieving 14.10× end-to-end inference acceleration on Wan2.1-1.3B and 8.89× speedup on CogVideoX-5B with quality improvement (VBench-2.0 from 0.534 to 0.569).

**Unified multimodal frontier models.** The architectural evolution has progressed from contrastive two-tower models (CLIP, Flamingo) to LLM-backbone adapters, then to native multimodal pretraining where image, video, audio, and text share a single trunk. Era 3b (unified multimodal input/output) sees models like Emu3 and Janus-Pro treating image, video, audio, and text as parts of one token or latent interface. GPT-4o accepts any combination of text, audio, image, and video input and generates any combination of text, audio, and image output, trained end-to-end across modalities with average response time of 232 milliseconds similar to human conversation. Ming-Omni is the first open-source model matching GPT-4o in modality support, perceiving text, images, videos, and audio while generating text, natural speech in real-time, and images simultaneously. Gemini 2.5 supports 1M-context window including interleaved images, audio, and video frames extended to ~10M tokens, with native audio generation and understanding with higher-rate tokenization sufficient for music understanding.

**Safety, ethics, and regulation.** The proliferation of realistic video generation has driven urgent policy responses. A U.S. Copyright Office report concluded a new federal law is needed for digital replicas due to the speed, precision, and scale of AI-created replicas threatening substantial harm, identifying three categories of unauthorized digital replicas: sexually explicit deepfake imagery, fraudulent activities (e.g., CEO voice impersonation scams), and political misinformation undermining democratic systems. The No AI FRAUD Act (H.R. 6943) proposed creating a federal framework protecting individual rights to likeness and voice against AI-generated counterfeits requiring written consent. California enacted AB 2839 targeting materially deceptive AI-generated deepfakes mimicking political candidates, though a judge ruled it facially violates First Amendment. Defenses against AI-generated media span detection (passive), disruption, and authentication (proactive) in a unified framework, with proactive defenses including adversarially perturbing inputs to prevent generation and injecting digital watermarks before release for traceability and copyright protection.

**Open research directions.** Autoregressive drift remains a persistent problem: BAgger introduces Backwards Aggregation, a self-supervised scheme constructing corrective trajectories from the model's own rollouts to teach recovery from exposure bias errors, addressing error accumulation in autoregressive video models where mismatch between training on clean contexts and inference on self-generated frames causes compounding errors. Few-step generation quality trade-offs persist — distilled models often sacrifice diversity and fine detail for speed. Scalable long-context video understanding remains unsolved, particularly for models needing to reason about events across minutes of generated content. IP infringement mitigation is critical as models trained on web-scale datasets incorporate copyrighted material. Finally, the gap between automated metrics and human perception remains wide: even the best MLLM-based evaluators achieve only moderate correlation with human judgments, and physics adherence shows particularly poor alignment with existing benchmarks.
